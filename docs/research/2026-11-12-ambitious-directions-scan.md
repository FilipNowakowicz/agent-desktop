# Ambitious directions: market and prior-art scan (2026-11-12)

**Status: a dated research record, not a plan.** This scan was run on 2026-11-12 to
look for prior art around "the computer as a repository" ideas: L1 desktop pull
requests, L2 forking, L3 earned autonomy and L4 shared paused desktops. Its claims
come from web research of varying quality: vendor documentation, preprints seen
partly as abstracts, and news reports. Nothing here was installed or reproduced.
"Not found" means not found in these searches, not proof of absence.

The scan recommends the desktop pull request as a flagship. The maintainer chose a
different direction on the same day: zero-touch delegation, proposed in
[DELEGATION.md](../project/DELEGATION.md). In that proposal the desktop pull request
and the honest final click are candidate features, not the direction. The scan is
kept because its sources are the prior-art record for several of that proposal's
ideas. The scan's labels L1–L4 are its own and are not rows of the
[execution plan](../project/PROJECT_PLAN.md#execution-plan-2026-11-11); its one
mention of "L5" and of "X4" does mean the plan's rows.

The original text follows. Only this header was added and the heading levels were
lowered by one; it contained no personal details to remove.

## Ship the desktop pull request first

**Every ingredient of "pull requests for computer work" (L1) already exists somewhere, but nobody has assembled them, and this project already owns most of the parts.** Confirmation gates before irreversible steps are universal. "Stop before the final click and keep the session live" is documented guidance (Steel) and a shipped pause/resume API (Browserbase, Anchor). Approval bound to the exact reviewed version, with an "outcome unknown means no blind retry" rule, ships in one SaaS-only product (hilos). Gating irreversible effects until commit is proven in research for tool calls (Atomix). Across five research passes, no product, open-source project or paper was found that combines a batched, asynchronous review of a *completed* desktop task, a diff of files, settings and app state, irreversible actions held in a live frozen desktop, reject-discards-everything, and a recorded delivered/unknown outcome for the final click. L2 (forking) is commodity in the cloud and a research prototype locally (TClone), and merging back into a person's real home directory is unclaimed. L3 (earned autonomy) is crowded as marketing, but in these searches nobody had shipped it as a working mechanism. L4 (sharing a paused desktop) has its transport solved by cloud browser vendors, and the reviewable, named-recipient object around it is missing. Among genuinely new directions, the strongest are a **trusted approval path the agent cannot see or forge**, **private evaluation of the user's own tasks**, and **desktop-level information-flow control**. The recommendation is to make L1 the flagship, with the trusted approval path built into it. Its first milestone is one fixture-backed "desktop PR" that passes five pass/fail gates: merge, reject, stale-review refusal, crash-to-unknown and leak counting. L1 is also the only direction that produces the review history L3 needs and the paused object L4 needs.

### Five shipped fragments add up to L1, but nobody has joined them

Every major computer-use product gates sensitive steps, and every one does it **synchronously, step by step, inside the running session**. Claude in Chrome offers per-action approval or an up-front plan approval, and still asks before purchases even on "always allow" sites ([Claude in Chrome permissions guide](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/12902446-claude-in-chrome-permissions-guide)). Google's Auto Browse "will stop before final checkout" ([TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/news/111112-google-introduces-agentic-ai-browsing-chrome-auto-browse.html?rand=98523)). Microsoft's Windows agent workspace, the closest in shape to this project (a separate agent account and desktop), "might request additional user approval" for sensitive steps and shows an action history ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/security/book/operating-system-agentic-security)). Microsoft Research's Magentic-UI adds "action guards" and co-planning in a VM sandbox ([arXiv 2507.22358](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.22358)). ChatGPT agent, the most visible takeover-and-confirm design, is now **no longer available** and points users to ChatGPT Work ([OpenAI Help](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/11752874-chatgpt-agent)). The plan approvals approve the work *before* it happens. None of these products reviews the *result* afterwards.

The brink pause itself is accepted practice. Steel's July 2026 guide prescribes exactly the L1 pattern: the worker "should stop before the final click or request", the browser stays live behind an interactive debug URL, the approval record carries a state checkpoint, and timeouts **fail closed** ([Steel](https://llms.steel.dev/articles/human-in-the-loop-browser-agents/)). It is guidance, though, and it does not address duplicate submission. Browserbase shipped `pauseWhen` → `PAUSED` → `resume` in September 2026, with no approve/reject semantics and no stated pause limit ([Browserbase changelog](https://browserbase.com/changelog/pause-and-resume-for-agents)). Anchor exposes a **queue of pending interventions** per session, the nearest shipped thing to an async review inbox ([Anchor HITL docs](https://docs.anchorbrowser.io/agentic-browser-control/human-in-the-loop)). All three are hosted Chromium only.

The closest product to L1's *semantics* is **hilos** (August 2026), which works through SaaS APIs rather than a desktop. It stages agent proposals against one Figma, Notion, Framer, Lovable or Paper resource and binds approval to the reviewed revision: if a Framer branch changed after review, the merge stops. It separates merge from publish, saves the human decision before the provider write, and when the outcome is unknown it refuses to reuse the approval until someone checks the provider ([hilos](https://hilos.sh/blog/review-ai-agent-work-without-pull-request)). That is this project's delivered/unknown stance applied to one resource at a time. L1 would apply the same contract to an arbitrary GUI desktop, where most apps have no branch or diff API. That is both the novelty and the hard part. Post-hoc products such as Rubrik Agent Rewind audit and restore after the fact rather than reviewing before a merge ([Computerworld](https://www.computerworld.com/article/4037957/ai-agents-make-mistakes-rubrik-has-figured-out-a-way-to-reverse-them.html)). HITL platforms such as HumanLayer and gotoHuman gate function calls through Slack or webhooks. They see no GUI, hold no live desktop and confirm nothing after approval ([HumanLayer](https://humanlayer.dev/docs/core/require-approval); [gotoHuman](https://docs.gotohuman.com/human-reviews)). They are notification plumbing L1 could use, not competitors.

#### Rollback cannot undo the outside world, which is why the brink matters

Undo literature converges on one limitation: **rollback restores local state but never sent mail, payments or remote services**. Berkeley's GoEx is the philosophical ancestor of "review the outcome, not the steps". It proposed post-facto validation, an undo abstraction and damage confinement for irreversible actions ([arXiv 2404.06921](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.06921)). DeltaBox reaches about **14 ms checkpoint and 5 ms rollback** for coding sandboxes ([arXiv 2605.22781](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22781)), and AgentRewind argues that agent context and environment must be reverted together ([arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.14380)). Neither targets full desktop GUI state. The conclusion for this project is structural. A post-hoc undo product is a weak bet. Holding the irreversible action until merge, and discarding a cheap local overlay on reject, is the right shape.

#### Exactly-once for a GUI click is achievable only as at-most-once plus observation

**Atomix** (MPI-SWS, Aarhus and EPFL, 2026) is the strongest theory for L1. It classes effects as reversible, bufferable or irreversible and holds irreversible ones at an adapter gate until commit. It leaked **0 of 500** invalid irreversible sends, against 80% for Saga and 40% for checkpoint-replay. It does **not claim crash-safe exactly-once**, it leaked 17 of 200 when adapter metadata was wrong, and GUI actions are "bypass paths" outside its enforcement boundary ([Atomix](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2602.14849)). Durable-execution engines (Temporal, Restate, DBOS, Inngest) journal steps and leave idempotency of non-idempotent effects to the user ([Pydantic AI durable execution](https://pydantic.dev/docs/ai/harness/durable-execution/); [Restate HITL](https://docs.restate.dev/ai/patterns/human-in-the-loop.md)). No paper or product was found on reconciling a GUI action after a crash by observing the screen or DOM.

The desktop runtime inverts Atomix's weakness. It **owns the input path**, so the GUI is the enforcement point rather than a bypass. The defensible claim is narrower than "exactly once": **at most once, then either observed confirmation (an order number, a sent-folder entry) or an explicit *unknown* that blocks retry**. That framing was not found claimed for GUI agents in these searches. It is also already the project's delivery vocabulary.

### Forking whole desktops is a cloud commodity, but merging back is unclaimed

Live forking with memory is now routine in hosted sandboxes. E2B forks capture "full filesystem and memory state", **up to 20 forks per call**, and drop WebSocket and PTY streams ([E2B fork docs](https://docs.e2b.dev/sandbox/fork)). Morph's Infinibranch snapshots and branches running instances ([Morph branch docs](https://cloud.morph.so/docs/documentation/instances/branch)). Fly Sprites roll one machine back through checkpoints rather than forking it N ways ([Fly checkpoint docs](https://fly.io/sprites/checkpoint-restore.md)), and a community thread reports checkpointing made Sprites unusable ([Fly community](https://community.fly.io/t/sprites-become-unusable-when-agents-checkpoint/27678)). Cua snapshots full desktop sandboxes on its cloud, but local snapshots are "limited" and unimplemented in cua-sandbox 0.7.0 ([Cua snapshots guide](https://cua.ai/docs/cua/guide/sandbox/snapshots)). All of this forks a *cloud VM* for parallel exploration or RL rollouts. **None of it merges a branch back into a user's real machine.**

The serious local prior art is **TClone** (UCSD WukLab, May 2026). It forks a containerised **Wayland** desktop with copy-on-write memory, extended CRIU and block-level copy-on-write files. It is up to **4.9× faster than KVM**, holds 16 clones in about 9 GB against about 14 GB for CRIU, runs on one i5 machine with 32 GB, and was evaluated on OSWorld with Agent S3 ([arXiv 2605.17320](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.17320v1)). Its paper *describes* promoting a branch or merging selected files with approval for conflicts, but shows **no merge implementation or evaluation**. Its "human" side is another container, not the person's real home. The released code needs **btrfs, root, amd64 and a custom kernel**, and has 3 stars and no releases ([GitHub os4agent](https://github.com/WukLab/os4agent)). BranchFS is a rootless FUSE copy-on-write filesystem. It creates branches in about 300 µs and commits atomically with **first-commit-wins**, so it has no merge at all, and it covers files only ([arXiv 2602.08199](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.08199v2)). Best-of-N over desktops works: Agent S3's behavior best-of-N lifts OSWorld from 62.6% to 69.9% ([arXiv 2510.02250](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.02250)) and Agent Alpha's MCTS reaches 77.29% ([arXiv 2602.02995](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.02995v1)). But both clone N cloud VMs from a fixed image, never the user's logged-in desktop.

Shipped merge-back stops at "review a diff, copy selected files back" through an overlay or git worktree ([Swival AgentFS](https://swival.dev/pages/agentfs.html); [Docker workshop](https://www.dockerworkshop.com/lab8/projects/reviewing-agent-changes/)). Some agent tools deliberately exclude `$HOME` from checkpoints ([Hermes docs](https://hermes-agent.nousresearch.com/docs/user-guide/checkpoints-and-rollback)). These searches found **no 3-way merge of non-code desktop state** (dconf, app configs, browser-profile SQLite) into a real home directory, and no product that branches a local desktop from a past moment. Building blocks such as Snapper `undochange` on a btrfs `/home` exist, but they are not agent-aware ([OneUptime Snapper guide](https://oneuptime.com/blog/post/2026-03-02-how-to-use-snapper-for-btrfs-snapshots-on-ubuntu/markdown)). L2 therefore splits in two. **File-level fork, discard and time travel** of a session home is feasible for a solo maintainer and is exactly what L1's reject path needs. **Live memory forking with semantic merge** is a kernel-research problem that TClone's authors have not finished. The name clash also gives positioning room: Vercel Labs' agent-desktop "snapshot" is a captured accessibility tree, with no fork or rollback ([flaviocopes](https://flaviocopes.com/agent-desktop)).

### Earned autonomy is crowded marketing, and shared paused desktops lack an object

For L3, the best evidence is that people *already* earn trust informally. Anthropic's telemetry shows Claude Code users moving from about **20% full auto-approve under 50 sessions to over 40% by about 750** ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/measuring-agent-autonomy)). Users approve **93%** of manual prompts, which led Anthropic to a classifier-based auto mode configured by rules. It does not learn from history, and the post notes one approval "isn't a pattern" ([Anthropic engineering](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/claude-code-auto-mode)). Vendors such as MightyBot, AgentAnchor and JieGou market graduated autonomy, but their internals could not be verified. The only open-source automatic promotion/demotion design is an unshipped RFC ([Paperclip #379](https://github.com/paperclipai/paperclip/issues/379)).

The research is fresh and close. Ou (May 2026) learns a per-user allow/block/ask boundary as a Gaussian-process posterior from binary approvals ([arXiv 2605.19151](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.19151)). Shimizu et al. (October 2026) find a shallow learner on **about 30–40 of one person's labels** beats every prompted model at predicting their auto-purchase line, and that **the boundary does not transfer across domains** ([arXiv 2610.29216](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29216)). That supports per-task-type grants. The warning comes from "Habituation at the Gate": across 16,895 reviews, approval rates rose while review effort fell ([arXiv 2606.22721](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.22721v1)). "48 of 50 merged unedited" can therefore mean fatigue rather than quality. A credible L3 weights edits and rejects above approvals, treats review effort as a signal, keeps spot-check sampling after a grant, and revokes on downstream reversals. No work was found applying any of this to computer-use agents. It is unclaimed, but it is **data-gated**: without L1's accept/edit/reject ledger there is nothing to learn from.

For L4, the transport is solved. Browserbase Live View is an embeddable, interactive URL meant to "share with users or coworkers" ([Browserbase docs](https://docs.browserbase.com/features/session-live-view)). Cloudflare's July 2026 **structured handoff** sends instructions, a Done/Failed result and an expiring live-view URL (5 minutes by default, up to 1 hour). It is the closest analogue to "send this paused browser to a colleague" ([Cloudflare HITL docs](https://developers.cloudflare.com/browser-run/features/human-in-the-loop)). Teleport's moderated sessions supply the role vocabulary of moderator, observer, peer and required participants, but for terminals ([Teleport docs](https://goteleport.com/docs/access-controls/guides/moderated-sessions)). What is missing everywhere is the **object**: a full desktop (not a browser tab) frozen before an irreversible step, carrying a record of what was done, granted to a named recipient with expiry, and closed by an audit entry. Back-office review today is forms and queues (UiPath Action Center), not the live app state ([UiPath docs](https://docs.uipath.com/maestro/automation-cloud/latest/user-guide/user-task)). L4 is novel, but its value depends on whether outside reviewers such as an accountant will open a live remote session at all. No source measured that.

### Three new directions beat the rest, and one of them belongs inside L1

The market pass found the money in hosted agents. Instinct raised **$1B at a $10B valuation** for a personal agent that uses "its own phone and computer" ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/09/28/everyday-personal-ai-assistant-startup-instinct-raises-1b-at-10b-valuation/)), and Daytona reached a $1B valuation for agent cloud ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/10/20/daytona-startup-building-the-agentic-cloud-valued-at-1-billion-in-latest-funding-round/)). Execution is moving off the user's machine: Claude Cowork moved its VM to the cloud in October 2026 and never had a Linux client ([Anthropic help center](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/14479288-claude-cowork-architecture-overview)). On 2026-11-12 the GitHub API reported 394,405 stars for [openclaw/openclaw](https://github.com/openclaw/openclaw), 32,252 for [trycua/cua](https://github.com/trycua/cua) and 22,629 for [vercel-labs/agent-desktop](https://github.com/vercel-labs/agent-desktop). The local, user-owned Linux lane is comparatively empty. The research pass found the frontier has moved past grounding. OSWorld 2.0's best binary completion is **20.6%**, and failures are constraint loss, missed mid-task information, guessing instead of asking, and skipped verification ([arXiv 2606.29537](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29537)). The open problems are trust boundaries, human interaction and realistic evaluation, and those are where this runtime's assets sit.

**A trusted approval path** is the most important new idea, because it fixes a hole in every L1 competitor. Agents can be induced to skip confirmation prompts ([arXiv 2507.05445](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.05445v1)), and Checkmarx's "Lies-in-the-Loop" forges approval dialogs so humans approve attacker content ([Infosecurity Magazine](https://www.infosecurity-magazine.com/news/lies-loop-attack-ai-safety-dialogs)). The CaMeL-for-computer-use authors call for intent provenance and runtime attestation because plan isolation does not stop "branch steering" ([arXiv 2601.09923](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.09923v1)). Here the approval would be rendered on the person's own screen, which the agent's private compositor cannot capture or click. It would be bound to a digest of the exact pending operation, and the runtime would release only that operation. In this project's architecture that is a **compositor-level fact, not a prompt instruction**. It is small, testable with a red-team suite, and it is what makes an L1 merge trustworthy. It belongs inside the flagship, not beside it.

**Own-task evaluation** is the cheapest credibility engine. Every major personal benchmark avoids real accounts by design: MyPCBench seeds one fictional persona ([arXiv 2606.16748](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16748)), and AgentCIBench warns its numbers are "not real-world risk rates" ([arXiv 2606.23189](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.23189)). Agents that succeed once fail on repeats ([arXiv 2604.17849](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.17849)). A local, opt-in record of each real job (instruction, trajectory, delivered/unknown labels, takeover segments and the human verdict) would let recurring read-only tasks be re-run against new models on real logins. The nearest neighbour replays WARC recordings and publishes them ([Personal-Agent-Benchmark](https://github.com/maxgent-ai/Personal-Agent-Benchmark)). Once L1 exists, its accept/edit/reject verdicts *are* these labels, so this direction comes almost free.

**Desktop-level information-flow control** carries the highest research value and the highest prior-art risk. The runtime would taint text by origin (a bank page, a tagged directory, a human paste) and refuse to type or fill it into another origin. That targets the leakage and visual-injection failures that system-prompt defenses miss ([VPI-Bench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.02456)). Prismata ([arXiv 2607.08147](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.08147)) and an execution-environment paper ([arXiv 2604.19657](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.19657)) were seen only as titles and may overlap, so no novelty claim can be made yet.

The remaining directions are weaker for a solo maintainer. **A safe substrate for personal agents** has the strongest pull and the weakest novelty. OpenClaw's one-click RCE (CVE-2026-25253) and exposed instances show demand for explicit, isolated sessions ([Hive Security](https://hivesecurity.gitlab.io/blog/openclaw-ai-agent-security-crisis-2026/)). This is a distribution channel for L1 rather than a flagship. **An air-gapped appliance** became possible because local computer-use weights exist (Holo3.1 GGUF, UI-TARS 7B) ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/monuminu/computer-use-agents-go-local-a-deep-technical-dive-into-on-device-gui-automation-quantized-2m3g)). But regulated buyers want vendors, and model licenses were not checked. **Nonvisual sessions for blind users** have real need: blind users' best model succeeded on **52.5%** of 1,258 commands, with breakdowns in feedback, confirmation and recovery ([arXiv 2609.00524](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00524)). A sighted solo maintainer cannot validate it without partners. **Takeover-as-correction** would record human rescues at the failure point as local in-context hints, but its novelty was never searched. A **portal-of-last-resort** agent for API-less portals and RDP-served legacy apps meets crowded Windows RPA incumbents.

The strongest objection to the flagship is the project's own record. The effect-first direction (D3) was marked weakened after its speed gates failed, and the R6 evidence-review experiment left "human review benefit unmeasured" (`docs/project/DIRECTIONS.md`, `docs/project/PROJECT_PLAN.md`). Those gates measured whether effect data made agents *faster*. L1 sells something else: the person never waits, and nothing irreversible happens without a bound decision. The milestone should therefore measure correctness and safety, not turns or seconds. A second objection is that Vercel or a cloud vendor could add review. Vercel's tool is an accessibility-tree CLI with no state capture, and cloud vendors cannot diff a person's own logged-in Linux home. The moat is locality plus honesty semantics, not UI.

### The first milestone is one desktop PR that passes five gates

The milestone, "M1: one desktop pull request", should run against fixtures only. An agent in a private session works with its home on a copy-on-write overlay (reflink copy or overlayfs, no CRIU and no custom kernel). It edits a document in a target directory, changes one gsettings key, and fills a compose form on a **local fixture mail server**. It then declares the brink by marking "Send" as pending through a new operation that the runtime also enforces for a small default list (send, submit, pay, delete). The runtime freezes the session, keeps the agent's input lease closed, and emits a review bundle containing the file diff, the dconf diff, a brink screenshot, the pending operation's target and a digest. The person decides with an out-of-band CLI command that the agent's session cannot see. It plugs into the existing approve/deny event API.

Five pass/fail gates decide the milestone. **Merge:** approval applies the overlay to the real target, delivers the click once, and records *delivered* only after the fixture shows a confirmation, with exactly one receipt at the fixture server. **Reject:** the real home and target are byte-identical afterwards, and the fixture server records zero receipts. **Stale review:** if the fixture page changes the recipient between review and merge, the digest mismatches and the merge is refused. **Crash:** if the runtime is killed between delivering the click and journaling it, the restart records *unknown*, never retries, and the fixture shows at most one receipt. **Leak count:** the fixture server counts any outbound request before approval, such as autosave or a background send, and the result is reported rather than hidden. Run 20 trials per gate and log them in the development log as experimental proof, not production reliability.

The milestone names the four honest risks up front. **Outbound leaks** bypass the brink, which is why the leak gate exists. **Brinks go stale** during long reviews through session or cart expiry, so merge must re-verify the screen and refuse rather than click blind. **Brink classification** is itself a judgement, so v1 uses explicit declaration plus a short enforced list and defers classifiers. And **"exactly once" is really at-most-once plus observation**, and the documentation should say so. A milestone that passes all five gates turns "computer as a repository" from a slogan into a contract that, in these searches, no one else had demonstrated.

### The stopped researchers left these questions open

The two interrupted notes are structurally complete, but they record specific unanswered questions. The approval-and-undo note did not verify Magentic-UI's action-guard semantics from the paper body. It found no first-party approval documentation for Manus, Simular, Cua or Anthropic's API computer-use tool. It did not check Permit.io, Cloudflare Agents approvals, LangGraph `interrupt` or the OpenAI Agents SDK's `needs_approval`. It did not verify SagaLLM, ALAS, AgentGit or "Speculative Actions" individually, and it could not say whether any tool diffs desktop settings (dconf/gsettings) for agent review, or whether that absence is real. The earned-autonomy-and-handoff note could not verify vendor trust-score internals (AgentAnchor, MightyBot, JieGou). It did not check OpenAI, Google or Microsoft adaptive-permission docs, CHI or CSCW 2026 proceedings, or RPA vendors beyond UiPath. It did not read the claims of a USPTO patent on "capabilities to increase autonomy" ([USPTO 12379948](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12379948)), which may matter for freedom to operate. It found nothing usable on Manus, Cua, Simular, Airtop or Copilot Actions handoff, and nothing on whether outside reviewers will accept a live remote session. Separately, the research-frontier pass hit a tool limit before fetching Prismata and arXiv 2604.19657 and before searching takeover-correction prior art, so the novelty of directions 6 and 10 in the table is unverified. None of these gaps changes the recommendation. The settings-diff and Magentic-UI gaps are the ones worth closing before claiming L1 novelty publicly.

### Conclusion

The research reframes the "computer as a repository" ladder as a **dependency chain, not a menu**. L1 produces the reviewed, bound, outcome-labelled records that L3 learns from, the frozen object that L4 shares, and the verdicts that own-task evaluation needs. Its reject path only requires file-level L2, not the kernel-level forking that stalls TClone. The competitive insight is that the cloud's advantages (forking, live-view URLs, pause APIs) are commodity, while this project's narrower assets are the scarce ones. Those assets are owning every input channel on the person's own machine, a compositor boundary the agent cannot see across, and an existing habit of saying *unknown* instead of retrying.

That changes what "ambitious" means for a 3-star solo project facing a 22k-star namesake. The flagship is a *contract* that larger projects have not offered for the desktop: nothing irreversible leaves the machine without a decision bound to what the person saw, and every outcome is recorded honestly. Beating them on breadth is not the goal. If M1's five gates pass, the project has a claim no competitor in these notes can make. If the leak gate fails badly, it has learned cheaply where desktop transactions break, which is itself publishable evidence.
